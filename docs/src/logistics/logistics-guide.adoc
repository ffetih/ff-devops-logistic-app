[preface]
== About this guide

`platform/ff-logistics/ff-logistics-app` is the logistics product: one deployable, built from modules that each own one part of the business.
This guide is for developers who change it: which modules exist, what each may use, and how its parts work.
How it is built, released and secured is in the link:{url-platform-guide}[Platform Guide]; how callers get a token is in the link:{url-identity-guide}[Identity Service Guide].

[[modules]]
== Modules

The app is a modular monolith. Spring Modulith treats every top-level package under `com.siose.ff.logistics` as a module, and `ModularityTest` holds them to three rules:

* *The base package is the module's API.* Other modules may use the public types in `com.siose.ff.logistics.<module>`; its sub-packages (`api`, `domain`, `service`) are internal.
* *Dependencies are declared.* `package-info.java` lists the modules it may use in `@ApplicationModule(allowedDependencies = ...)`. Anything else fails the build.
* *No cycles.* If two modules need each other, one of them publishes an event instead (<<events>>).

A module that keeps these rules can later move into a service of its own without untangling it from the rest: its callers already use only its API.

[plantuml,logistics-modules,svg]
----
@startuml
skinparam componentStyle rectangle
package "logistics-app" {
  [caller] as caller
  [release] as release
  [standards] as standards
  [wms] as wms
  [tms] as tms
  [geo] as geo
  [reporting] as reporting
}
wms --> standards
tms --> standards
geo --> standards
reporting ..> tms : events
@enduml
----

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Module | May use | Owns

| `caller` | nothing | `GET /api/v1/me`: the tenant, user and roles the app took from the token
| `release` | nothing | The `releaseSwitch` health contributor read by the canary analysis
| `standards` | nothing | GS1 check digits, ISO 6346 container codes, EN 16258 emissions, great-circle distances (<<standards>>)
| `wms` | `standards` | Warehouses, bin locations, inbound ASNs (<<wms>>)
| `tms` | `standards` | Shipments with their legs, e-CMR number and CO~2~e footprint (<<tms>>)
| `geo` | `standards` | Territories, and route plans with their stops in driving order (<<geo>>)
| `reporting` | `tms` (its events only) | Its own copy of delivered shipments, filled from `ShipmentDelivered` (<<events>>)
|===

The base libraries (`siose-ff-base-*`) are not modules of the app; every module may use them.

[[standards]]
== Standards

`com.siose.ff.logistics.standards` holds the public standards the other modules build on, as pure functions: static methods, no state, no beans, no database.
A rule that a GS1 or ISO document defines is written once, here, and tested against the examples in that document.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Class | Standard | Used for

| `Gs1Standards` | GS1 General Specifications: Modulo-10 check digit | Warehouse and site numbers (GLN-13), trade items (GTIN), pallet labels (SSCC-18)
| `Iso6346ContainerCode` | ISO 6346: owner code, category, serial, Modulo-11 check digit | Containers and trailers on a transport leg
| `EmissionsCalculator`, `TransportMode` | EN 16258 (now ISO 14083) and the GLEC Framework | CO~2~e of a shipment and the saving against road only
| `GeoDistance` | Great-circle distance (haversine), on a sphere of the mean earth radius | Road distance estimates for route plans (<<geo>>)
|===

=== GS1 check digit

Every GS1 key ends in the same check digit.
From the right, the digits are weighted 3, 1, 3, 1, ...; the check digit brings the weighted sum up to the next multiple of ten.
Starting from the right is what lets one rule serve keys of every length.

[source,text]
----
GLN  0 6 1 4 1 4 1 0 0 0 0 1 | ?
x    1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3      (weights from the right: 3, 1, 3, ...)
sum  0+18+1+12+1+12+1+0+0+0+0+3 = 48   ->  next multiple of 10 is 50   ->  check digit 2
     0614141000012
----

An SSCC is the extension digit, the GS1 company prefix, a serial reference and the check digit, 18 digits in all.
The longer the company prefix, the fewer digits remain for the serial: a 7-digit prefix leaves 9, so one prefix numbers a billion pallets per extension digit.
`generateSscc18` refuses a serial that does not fit rather than cutting it.

=== ISO 6346 container code

[source,text]
----
C S Q U   3 0 5 4 3 8   3
└─owner─┘ └─serial──┘   check digit
      └ category: U container, J detachable equipment, Z trailer or chassis
----

Each of the first ten characters gets a value (digits their own; letters from A = 10 upwards, skipping 11, 22 and 33, so L = 23 and V = 34), multiplied by 2 to the power of its position.
The check digit is the sum modulo 11, with a remainder of 10 written as 0.

=== Emissions

For every leg of a transport chain: distance x weight x the mode's factor, in grams of CO~2~e per tonne-km, added up.
`EmissionsCalculator.footprint` also returns what the same cargo would have emitted by road all the way, because that is the comparison a customer makes when it moves part of the route to rail or sea.

[cols="2,1",options="header"]
|===
| `TransportMode` | g CO~2~e per tonne-km

| `ROAD` | 62
| `RAIL` | 22
| `SEA_RORO` | 31
| `INLAND_WATERWAY` | 28
| `AIR` | 602
|===

The factors are typical well-to-wheel averages for a practice project, not certified figures: a real carrier uses its own measured values.
For 22 t from Istanbul by Ro-Ro to Trieste (1450 km), block train to Cologne (1050 km) and 80 km by truck, the chain emits 1606.22 kg against 3519.12 kg by road only, 54.36 % less.

[[wms]]
== Warehouse management (WMS)

The WMS module knows where goods can be and what is about to arrive.
Its tables start with `wms_`; every row carries the tenant, and Hibernate adds `tenant_id = ?` to every query, so the repositories never mention a tenant and one tenant's warehouse is, for another tenant, simply not there (`404`).

[plantuml,wms-model,svg]
----
@startuml
hide circle
hide methods
skinparam classAttributeIconSize 0
class Warehouse {
  {field} code (unique per tenant)
  name
  {field} gln : GLN-13 (unique per tenant)
  gs1CompanyPrefix
  countryCode, city, latitude, longitude
  palletCapacity
}
class BinLocation {
  {field} code, e.g. A02-R11-L04 (unique per warehouse)
  kind : RACK | FLOOR | STAGING
  palletCapacity
}
class Asn {
  {field} asnNumber (unique per tenant)
  supplierName
  expectedArrival
  status : EXPECTED | RECEIVING | RECEIVED
  version
}
class AsnLine {
  lineNumber
  gtin : GTIN-8/12/13/14
  skuCode
  expectedQuantity
  receivedQuantity
}
class Pallet {
  {field} sscc : SSCC-18 (unique everywhere)
  gtin, skuCode
  quantity
  grossWeightKg
}
Warehouse "1" *-- "*" BinLocation
Warehouse "1" <-- "*" Asn : delivered to
Asn "1" *-- "1..*" AsnLine
Asn "1" <-- "*" Pallet : received from
BinLocation "1" <-- "*" Pallet : stands in
@enduml
----

Warehouse:: A warehouse, cross-dock or hub. Its GLN is the number partners know it by. The GLN must start with the GS1 company prefix, because a GS1 member numbers its own locations from its own prefix; the same prefix will start every pallet label printed there.
Bin location:: One addressable place inside a warehouse. The code is what is printed on the label at the place and what a scanner reads.
ASN (advance shipping notice):: The supplier's message that goods are on their way: EDIFACT DESADV, GS1 despatch advice. One line per GTIN, numbered from 1. The ASN and its lines are one aggregate: lines are only created and changed through the ASN, which keeps the rules in one place.
Pallet:: Goods of one ASN line, built into a pallet at receiving, labelled with an SSCC and put away into a bin location. From then on the SSCC is the pallet's identity for everyone in the chain.

=== API

All endpoints need `WAREHOUSE_OPERATOR` or `TENANT_ADMIN` (<<roles>>). Errors are RFC 9457 problems; the `type` says which rule was broken.

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
| Request | Does | Refused with

| `POST /api/v1/wms/warehouses` | Registers a warehouse; code and country are stored upper case | `400 domain-validation` (GLN check digit, prefix), `409 warehouse-code-taken`, `409 warehouse-gln-taken`
| `GET /api/v1/wms/warehouses`, `/{id}` | The tenant's warehouses, by code | `404` for another tenant's id
| `POST /api/v1/wms/warehouses/{id}/bins` | Adds a bin location | `409 bin-code-taken`
| `GET /api/v1/wms/warehouses/{id}/bins` | The warehouse's bin locations, by code |
| `POST /api/v1/wms/asns` | Records an ASN with its lines; status `EXPECTED` | `400 domain-validation` (GTIN check digit, a GTIN twice), `404` unknown warehouse, `409 asn-number-taken`
| `GET /api/v1/wms/asns`, `/{id}` | The receiving plan: by expected arrival, then number |
| `POST /api/v1/wms/asns/{id}/pallets` | Receives one pallet: books the quantity on the GTIN's line, labels it with a new SSCC, puts it into the bin (<<receiving>>) | `400 domain-validation` (GTIN not on the ASN, bin not in its warehouse), `409 over-receipt`, `409 asn-already-received`, `409 bin-full`
| `GET /api/v1/wms/asns/{id}/pallets` | The pallets received against an ASN, by SSCC |
| `GET /api/v1/wms/pallets/{sscc}` | What a scanner asks when it reads a pallet label | `404` for another tenant's pallet
|===

[[receiving]]
=== Receiving and SSCC labels

One request is one pallet, the way a receiving clerk works: scan the goods, count them onto a pallet, print the label, scan the bin.

[plantuml,wms-receiving,svg]
----
@startuml
start
:lock the ASN (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE);
:lock the bin location;
if (bin already full?) then (yes)
  :409 bin-full;
  stop
endif
if (ASN received / GTIN not on it / more than the line still expects?) then (yes)
  :409 or 400;
  stop
endif
:book the quantity on the line;\nASN -> RECEIVING, or RECEIVED when no line is open;
:next value of wms_sscc_serial_seq;
:SSCC = 0 + company prefix + serial + check digit;
:save the pallet;
:201 with the SSCC;
stop
@enduml
----

[source,text]
----
0 8690001 000100001 0
│ └──┬──┘ └───┬───┘ └ check digit (GS1 Modulo-10)
│    │        └ serial reference: wms_sscc_serial_seq, 9 digits after a 7-digit prefix
│    └ GS1 company prefix of the warehouse operator (the warehouse's gs1CompanyPrefix)
└ extension digit: always 0 here
----

Why the serial comes from a database sequence:: An SSCC must never be issued twice, and GS1 forbids reusing one within a year of shipping the pallet. A PostgreSQL sequence hands out each value once, across restarts and across every pod of the app; a counter in memory would repeat numbers after a restart, and "highest so far plus one" would hand the same number to two requests at the same moment.
A serial is not given back when the request fails afterwards, so the numbers can have gaps. A gap is allowed; a number used twice is not.

Why the ASN is locked:: Two scanners can receive against the same ASN at once. Without a lock, both would read "2 units still open", both would book 2, and the line would show 12 of 10. `findForReceivingById` takes a row lock until the transaction ends, so the second request waits, then sees 0 open and gets `409`. The bin is locked the same way, for its last free place, and always after the ASN, so two requests can never wait for each other.


[[tms]]
== Transport management (TMS)

The TMS module plans shipments: one piece of equipment, carrying goods labelled with an SSCC, from origin to destination over a chain of transport legs.
Its tables start with `tms_`, and like every table they are filtered by tenant.

[plantuml,tms-model,svg]
----
@startuml
hide circle
hide methods
skinparam classAttributeIconSize 0
class Shipment {
  {field} shipmentNumber : SHP-10001 (from tms_shipment_seq)
  {field} customerOrderReference (unique per tenant)
  {field} ecmrNumber : ECMR-10001
  consignorName, consigneeName
  {field} equipmentCode : ISO 6346
  {field} sscc : SSCC-18
  {field} incoterm : Incoterms 2020
  origin, destination
  grossWeightKg
  {field} footprint : distance, CO2e, road only, saved, %
  {field} status : see the lifecycle
  pickedUpAt, departedAt, deliveredAt
  receivedBy
  version
}
class TransportLeg {
  sequenceNo
  {field} mode : SEA_RORO | RAIL | ROAD | ...
  origin, destination
  distanceKm
  carrierReference
}
Shipment "1" *-- "1..*" TransportLeg : route, in order
@enduml
----

Shipment:: The consignment as the planner books it. The shipment and its legs are one aggregate; the legs are only created through the shipment.
e-CMR number:: The number of the electronic consignment note. The CMR convention's waybill travels with every international road transport in Europe; e-CMR is its electronic form. One per shipment here, numbered with the shipment.
Equipment:: The container or trailer, by its ISO 6346 code. A wrong check digit is refused, so a typo cannot book the wrong box.
Incoterm:: Who pays which part of the journey and where the risk passes, from the sales contract (Incoterms 2020: `EXW` … `DDP`).
Route:: The legs in order. The first starts at the shipment's origin, each next one where the previous ended, the last ends at the destination; a gap is refused with the leg that breaks the chain.
Footprint:: EN 16258 from `standards`, calculated when the shipment is planned and kept: a later change of emission factors does not rewrite what a customer was told.

[[customer-order]]
=== One shipment per customer order number

A client that sends `POST /shipments` and gets no answer, a timeout, a dropped connection, cannot know whether the shipment was created. With a `customerOrderReference`, its own order number, it can simply send the same request again:

[cols="3,2",options="header"]
|===
| The order number is | Answer

| not given | `201`, a new shipment every time
| new for this tenant | `201`, the new shipment
| known, and the plan is the same | `200`, the shipment created the first time
| known, but with another plan | `409 customer-order-conflict`: another order needs another number
|===

The hard case is two such retries arriving at the same moment: both look, both find nothing, both insert. The unique index `(tenant_id, customer_order_reference)` lets only one insert commit. The other fails, its transaction is rolled back, and a second, fresh transaction reads the winner and answers `200` with it. Eight identical requests in parallel against PostgreSQL get one `201` and seven `200`, all for the same shipment.

Every losing insert had already taken a shipment number, so the numbers then have gaps (`SHP-10001`, then `SHP-10010`). A gap is allowed; a number given twice is not.

[[shipment-lifecycle]]
=== Lifecycle

[plantuml,tms-lifecycle,svg]
----
@startuml
hide empty description
[*] --> PLANNED : POST /shipments
PLANNED --> PICKED_UP : POST …/pickup
PICKED_UP --> DEPARTED : POST …/departure
PLANNED --> DEPARTED : POST …/departure\n(pickup not reported)
DEPARTED --> DELIVERED : POST …/delivery\n{"receivedBy": …}
DELIVERED --> [*]
@enduml
----

A shipment only moves forward. A step may be left out, because not every carrier reports every scan, but never repeated or taken back: a pickup after the departure answers `409 shipment-status-conflict`. Each step records its time (`pickedUpAt`, `departedAt`); a step that was left out keeps no time.

The delivery records who signed for the goods at the consignee, the name under the e-CMR's "goods received", and is the end: nothing comes after it.

[[delivered-once]]
==== Delivered once, also at the same moment

Two drivers' apps can confirm the same delivery within milliseconds. The lifecycle alone would not stop that: both requests read `DEPARTED`, both see that `DELIVERED` comes next, both write. The shipment therefore carries a `version`, and Hibernate makes every update conditional on it:

[source,text]
----
request A                                     request B
read SHP-10001: DEPARTED, version 1           read SHP-10001: DEPARTED, version 1
UPDATE … SET status='DELIVERED', version=2
  WHERE id=… AND version=1      → 1 row
commit                                        UPDATE … SET status='DELIVERED', version=2
                                                WHERE id=… AND version=1   → 0 rows
200 DELIVERED                                 409 concurrent-update (nothing changed)
----

A confirmation that arrives after the first has committed reads `DELIVERED` and is refused by the lifecycle instead (`409 shipment-status-conflict`). Either way the shipment is delivered exactly once; with four confirmations sent in parallel against PostgreSQL, one answers `200` and three `409 concurrent-update`.

This is optimistic locking: nothing is locked while a request works, and a conflict is found at the write. The WMS uses the other kind for receiving (<<receiving>>): a row lock that makes the second scanner wait. A wait suits receiving, where the second pallet is still valid and only has to come after the first; a refusal suits delivery, where the second confirmation is a duplicate.

=== API

All endpoints need `LOGISTICS_PLANNER` or `TENANT_ADMIN` (<<roles>>).

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
| Request | Does | Refused with

| `POST /api/v1/tms/shipments/{id}/pickup`, `/departure` | Moves the shipment one step on (<<shipment-lifecycle>>) | `409 shipment-status-conflict`
| `POST /api/v1/tms/shipments/{id}/delivery` | Delivers it, with `{"receivedBy": "…"}` (<<delivered-once>>) | `409 shipment-status-conflict`, `409 concurrent-update`
| `POST /api/v1/tms/shipments` | Plans a shipment; numbers it, calculates the footprint; status `PLANNED`. `201`, or `200` with the existing shipment for a known `customerOrderReference` and the same plan (<<customer-order>>) | `400 domain-validation` (ISO 6346 or SSCC check digit, a broken route), `400 invalid-payload` (unknown incoterm or mode), `409 customer-order-conflict`
| `GET /api/v1/tms/shipments`, `/{id}` | The tenant's shipments, newest first | `404` for another tenant's id
|===

[[geo]]
== Geo: territories and route plans

The geo module answers a dispatcher's question for one van and one day: in which order should it drive its stops, and when will it be where?
Its tables start with `geo_`, and like every table they are filtered by tenant.

[plantuml,geo-model,svg]
----
@startuml
hide circle
hide methods
skinparam classAttributeIconSize 0
class Territory {
  {field} territoryCode (unique per tenant)
  name
  {field} hubGln : GLN-13
  centerLatitude, centerLongitude
  coverageRadiusKm
  activePointCount, weeklyVisitTarget
  {field} workloadBalanceScore : 70 … 100
}
class RoutePlan {
  {field} routeNumber : RTE-5001 (from geo_route_seq)
  territoryCode
  vehiclePlate
  depotLatitude, depotLongitude
  vehicleCapacityKg, averageSpeedKmh
  loadedWeightKg, capacityUtilizationPct
  totalDistanceKm, totalMinutes
}
class RouteStop {
  sequenceNo
  stopCode, customerName
  latitude, longitude
  demandWeightKg, serviceMinutes
  {field} readyMinutes, dueMinutes : time window, optional
  {field} legDistanceKm : from the stop before
  {field} arrivalMinutes : after departure
}
Territory "1" .. "0..*" RoutePlan : by code
RoutePlan "1" *-- "1..*" RouteStop : in driving order
@enduml
----

Territory:: The area one hub serves: a circle around a centre, with the delivery points in it and the visits a week they need. The hub's GLN must have a valid check digit (`BR-GEO-01`). The workload balance score is 70, plus up to 20 for density (points per km of radius) and up to 10 for visit frequency (5 per weekly visit per point), at most 100 (`BR-GEO-02`).
Route plan:: One vehicle's tour from a depot, numbered `RTE-…`. It belongs to a territory of the tenant, by code. The tour ends at the last stop: the way back to the depot is not part of it.
Route stop:: A delivery point, with the load it takes off the vehicle, how long the vehicle stays and, if the customer has one, a time window: the earliest and the latest arrival in minutes after departure. The plan gives it its place in the order, the distance from the stop before and the arrival time, in minutes after departure.

=== Planning a route

[plantuml,geo-planning,svg]
----
@startuml
start
:at most 99 stops? (BR-GEO-07);
note right: before any work: ordering grows\nwith the square of the list
:the territory exists for this tenant?;
:every stop code once? (BR-GEO-06)\nload within the capacity? (BR-GEO-03)\ntime windows whole?;
:road distances and drive times\nbetween every pair of points (BR-GEO-05);
if (a time window, or more than 8 stops?) then (yes)
  :Timefold: every stop on time,\nthen the shortest distance;
  if (every stop on time?) then (no)
    :400;
    stop
  else (yes)
  endif
else (no)
  :nearest neighbour;
endif
:arrival at each stop: the stop before\n+ its service + the drive, waiting\nfor a window that is not open yet;
:number the plan, save it;
stop
@enduml
----

Each check that fails answers `400` and names what is wrong. A refused plan takes no route number.

Stop codes (`BR-GEO-06`):: Each stop's proof of delivery will be filed under its code (S07-06). Codes are compared as a file name is made from them, in lower case and with anything other than letters, digits and hyphens as a hyphen, so `STOP_1`, `stop 1` and `STOP-1` are one code and refused together.
Road distance (`BR-GEO-05`):: The straight line between two points times 1.24, the usual detour factor for road freight in Europe, and the drive time at the requested average speed (10 to 130 km/h), rounded up to whole minutes. With an OSRM server configured, distance and drive time come from the road network instead (<<osrm>>).
Order (`BR-GEO-04`):: Up to 8 stops and no time window: nearest neighbour, from the depot always to the closest stop not yet visited. Otherwise Timefold (<<time-windows>>). A vehicle that arrives before a window opens waits; one that would arrive after it closes is not allowed.
Limit (`BR-GEO-07`):: `GEO_MAX_STOPS`, 99 by default.

[[time-windows]]
=== Time windows: Timefold

Nearest neighbour looks one stop ahead. With time windows that is not enough: a far stop that closes early has to come first, although a near one is waiting.
https://timefold.ai[Timefold] searches orders instead: it builds one, then keeps changing it (moving a stop, swapping two, reversing a stretch) and keeps a change when the score improves.

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Level | Penalty | Means

| Hard | One point per minute any stop is late | Any on-time order beats every late one
| Soft | One point per metre of road | Among on-time orders, the shortest wins
|===

The score is calculated with the same clock as the plan: drive, wait if early, serve, drive on (`Tour.latenessMinutes`).
If the best order the solver finds is still late, the route is refused with `400`: no order of these stops keeps every window, or none that the solver found.

The solver stops when its best order has not improved for 0.2 s (`solver-unimproved-limit`), and at the latest after 1 s (`solver-limit`). A small route is answered in a fraction of a second; a hard one cannot hold the request longer. Lists over 8 stops go to Timefold too, without windows: there nearest neighbour's mistakes add up.

[[osrm]]
=== Road distances from OSRM

https://project-osrm.org[OSRM] routes on OpenStreetMap data. Its table service answers, in one request, the road distance and drive time between every pair of a route's points, where the estimate only knows the straight line.
It needs a map prepared in advance, so the cluster does not run one; the estimate is the default everywhere.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Meaning

| `GEO_ROAD_MATRIX` | `haversine` (default) or `osrm`
| `GEO_OSRM_URL` | The OSRM server, `http://localhost:5000` by default
|===

What the client does, and why (`OsrmRoadMatrix`):

* *Timeouts:* 2 s to connect, 5 s to answer. A stalled OSRM would otherwise hold the route request as long as the caller waits. A timeout, no server, a refused table, or two points with no road between them all answer `503 road-matrix-unavailable`, and no plan is stored.
* *Coordinates in the path as they are:* `…/table/v1/driving/28.65,41.1;28.67,41.03`. Spring would encode `,` and `;` as URI variables, and OSRM rejects the encoded form.
* *No compression:* OSRM's deflate answer is not what Spring's inflater expects. The answers are small.
* *Size:* OSRM refuses a table above its `--max-table-size`, 100 points by default: the 99 stops of `GEO_MAX_STOPS` plus the depot.

To try it from the IDE, prepare a map and start a server (Docker or Podman; Istanbul cut out of Geofabrik's Turkey extract with `osmium extract --bbox 28.4,40.8,29.5,41.3`):

[source,bash]
----
IMG=ghcr.io/project-osrm/osrm-backend
docker run --rm -v "$PWD:/data" $IMG osrm-extract -p /opt/car.lua /data/istanbul.osm.pbf
docker run --rm -v "$PWD:/data" $IMG osrm-partition /data/istanbul.osrm
docker run --rm -v "$PWD:/data" $IMG osrm-customize /data/istanbul.osrm
docker run --rm -p 5000:5000 -v "$PWD:/data" $IMG osrm-routed --algorithm mld /data/istanbul.osrm
----

Then run the app with `GEO_ROAD_MATRIX=osrm`. The walkthrough's route keeps its order but its distances become the roads'.

[[events]]
== Events between modules

When something happens in one module that another cares about, the first publishes an event and does not know who listens. TMS publishes `ShipmentDelivered` when a consignee signs; reporting listens and keeps its own record of delivered shipments. TMS calls nothing in reporting, and reporting reads no TMS table: `ModularityTest` allows `reporting` to use `tms` for its events only, which are the records in the `tms` base package.

=== The outbox: an event cannot get lost

The event must not get lost, and must not be sent for a delivery that did not happen. Spring Modulith's event publication registry gives both, by writing the event into the database in the same transaction as the change:

[plantuml,shipment-delivered,svg]
----
@startuml
participant "POST …/delivery" as api
participant "TMS\nShipmentService" as tms
database "PostgreSQL" as db
participant "reporting\nlistener (async)" as rep
api -> tms : deliver
activate tms
tms -> db : UPDATE tms_shipments … status DELIVERED, version+1
tms -> db : INSERT event_publication\n(ShipmentDelivered as JSON, listener, PUBLISHED)
tms -> db : COMMIT  (both, or neither)
deactivate tms
tms --> api : 200
db -> rep : after the commit, on its own thread
activate rep
rep -> db : INSERT rep_shipment_deliveries (unless already there)
rep -> db : event_publication: COMPLETED
deactivate rep
@enduml
----

* *Both or neither.* The event row commits with the delivery. A confirmation that loses on the version (<<delivered-once>>) is rolled back with its event, so four parallel confirmations leave one delivered shipment and one event.
* *Committed means it will arrive.* The listener runs after the commit, on a thread of its own, and the row stays open until it has succeeded. If the listener throws, or the pod stops first, the row stays open and the event is delivered again (<<resubmission>>).
* *At least once, so recorded once.* An event can therefore arrive twice. Reporting ignores a shipment it already has, and the table has a unique `shipment_id` besides.
* *The event carries what the listener needs*, including the tenant: a listener has no request and so no tenant of its own. It binds the tenant from the event before its transaction starts.

[source,sql]
----
-- what the registry has, newest first: one row per event and listener
select listener_id, status, completion_attempts, publication_date, completion_date
from event_publication order by publication_date desc limit 10;
----

`ShipmentDelivered` is stored as JSON until it is handled, possibly across a release: fields may be added to it, never renamed or removed.

[[resubmission]]
=== Events a listener has not handled

A row that stays open is an event still owed to a listener. The `outbox` module, a technical module of its own, publishes such events again:

[source,text]
----
listener throws                      → row FAILED at once
pod stopped while the listener ran   → row stays PUBLISHED / PROCESSING,
                                       turns FAILED after 5 min (spring.modulith.events.staleness.*)

every 30 s, in every pod:
  pg_try_advisory_lock(5105000001)   → only one pod gets it; the others skip this run
  FAILED rows that are due           → published again; the listener runs again
  pg_advisory_unlock
----

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Why

| One pod at a time, by a PostgreSQL advisory lock | Four pods run the same schedule. Without the lock, all four would publish the same row at once, and the listener would run four times.
| The wait doubles: 1 min, 2, 4, 8, up to 1 h (`EventRetryPolicy`) | A listener that fails because something it needs is down gets time; a quick retry would fail the same way.
| At most 5 attempts, then the row stays open | A listener that keeps failing has a bug or bad data. Retrying forever only adds load; the gauge `logistics.events.publications.exhausted` and a warning in the log say a person is needed.
| Not at a pod's start (`republish-outstanding-events-on-restart: false`) | Every pod would publish everything open at its start.
| Completed rows are deleted after 7 days | The table would otherwise grow with every event.
|===

The settings are in `application.yml` under `logistics.events` and `spring.modulith.events`; the cluster can override them by environment (`EVENTS_RESUBMIT_DELAY`, `EVENTS_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, `EVENTS_STALE_AFTER`). What to do with a row that stopped retrying is in the Operations Guide.

=== Listeners do not run without limit

Listeners run on virtual threads, on an executor that has no limit by default. A burst of deliveries would start every listener at once, all waiting for one of the ten database connections. `spring.task.execution.simple.concurrency-limit` (16, `TASK_EXECUTION_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT`) caps them; above it, the publisher waits for a free slot.
